A short summary of MSET
=======================

MSET is a global economic model. It is used to study how climate change, 
climate policy, and other policies affect countries, industries, jobs, prices, 
trade, and emissions.

The model starts with assumptions about the world. These can include taxes,
government spending, consumer demand, exports, interest rates, technology, 
and population.

From those assumptions, the model works out:

- how much each industry produces,
- how many people are employed,
- prices and wages,
- how goods and money move between regions,
- how much energy is used,
- how much pollution and greenhouse gas emissions are produced.

MSET solves the economy one year at a time. The result for one year becomes the
starting point for the next year. This is why the model is referred to as 
recursively dynamic.

Regions and sectors 
-------------------

MSET separates the world into regions, with each region containing many sectors.
The model resolution is flexible, meaning that the level of sectoral and 
regional detail can be adjusted depending on the project. 

Input-output structure
----------------------

At the core of MSET is a multi-regional input-output framework. This tracks what
each sector buys from other sectors, including sectors in other regions. 
If demand for one product rises, the model can follow the knock-on effects 
through the supply chain.

The model combines this with equations for things like household demand, 
investment, government spending, trade, employment, and prices.

Calibration
-----------

Calibration means adjusting the model so that it fits known or projected data 
for a base year or period.

The calibration process saves final-demand residuals. Later scenario runs can 
reuse these residuals so that scenarios start from the same baseline.

More detail on the yearly solving process can be found in :doc:`model_solving`.
